407 is the project. Not how concentrated the market is today, but how much of it thinned while nobody was counting. Reconstructed from every product Health Canada has ever authorised, not inferred from a snapshot.

We do not headline 58.9 %

It is not ours and not new. PMPRB / Gaudette et al. (JAMA Health Forum 2024) report 52 % of off-patent drugs in a monopoly market in 2022. Zhang et al. (CMAJ Open 2020) reach ≈51 %. We differ because we count active-ingredient sets, and because company_name is the authorisation holder, not the plant. Merging parents could only lower our number.

One supplier is not a shortage warning

PMPRB's 2022 shortage study: of 8,558 shortage reports only 2 % were single-source non-patented drugs, and single-source drugs were in shortage 22 % of the time against 32 % for multi-source. Santhireswaran et al. (JAMA Netw Open 2026) found drugs with more than 25 products available had the higher shortage intensity. If this page implied a supply warning, the regulator would contradict it. It does not.

Finished vs planned

Finished: full pipeline from a clean clone; 25 checks at a fixed denominator; 25 planted defects, 25 caught, 0 missed; every figure here re-derived by a check, none typed by hand.
Not done: no shortage data (healthproductshortages.ca needs an account we did not create); rx reads today's value because the database has no point-in-time status; all cohorts share one endpoint and overlap heavily, so this is three views of one outcome, not three replications; no therapeutic-substitution check, so we cannot separate obsolescence from loss.

The finding we deleted

Molecules with more suppliers disappear less often. That gradient holds in all three cohorts and it looks like a finding. It is arithmetic. A molecule leaves the market only when every supplier leaves, so if companies depart independently at rate p, the rate falls as pk with no signal at all.
